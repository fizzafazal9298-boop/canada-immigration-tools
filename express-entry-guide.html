Express Entry, step by step

Express Entry is Canada's online system for three skilled immigration programs. You create a profile, receive a CRS score, and — if you rank high enough in a draw — get an Invitation to Apply (ITA) for permanent residence.

The three programs

ProgramWho it is for
Federal Skilled Worker (FSW)Skilled workers with foreign work experience. You must first score at least 67/100 on the separate FSW selection grid (language, education, experience, age, arranged employment, adaptability) before CRS even applies.
Canadian Experience Class (CEC)Workers with at least one year of skilled work experience in Canada in the last three years.
Federal Skilled Trades (FST)Qualified tradespeople with a certificate of qualification or a qualifying job offer in a skilled trade.

The process

  1. Check eligibility for at least one program (FSW candidates: the 67-point grid first).
  2. Take an approved language test — IELTS General Training, CELPIP-General or PTE Core for English; TEF Canada or TCF Canada for French. Results are converted to CLB/NCLC levels.
  3. Get an Educational Credential Assessment (ECA) if your degree or diploma is from outside Canada.
  4. Create your Express Entry profile. You enter the pool and receive your CRS score out of 1,200.
  5. Wait for a draw — and improve meanwhile. Retake a language test, complete another credential, or pursue a provincial nomination while you wait.
  6. Receive an ITA and submit your full permanent residence application, normally within 60 days.
  7. Complete medicals, biometrics and background checks, then receive your confirmation of permanent residence.

Category-based draws

Alongside general draws, IRCC runs category-based rounds for candidates with specific strengths. Categories used in recent years include French-language proficiency, healthcare and social services occupations, STEM occupations, trades, transport, and agriculture and agri-food occupations. If you fit a category, your effective cut-off can be far lower than the general one — French speakers especially.

2025 rule change: CRS points for arranged employment (job offers) were removed on 25 March 2025. A job offer can still matter for program eligibility and some provincial streams, but it no longer adds 50 or 200 points to your score. Be careful with older guides and calculators that still include it — ours does not.

Documents most people need

  • Passport and identity documents
  • Language test results (less than 2 years old)
  • ECA report for foreign education
  • Reference letters proving skilled work experience (NOC/TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3)
  • Proof of settlement funds (for FSW and FST, unless exempt)
  • Police certificates and medical exam results after your ITA
