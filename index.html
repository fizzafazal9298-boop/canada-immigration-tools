What score do you need?

There is no fixed pass mark. IRCC invites the highest-ranked candidates in each draw, and cut-offs move with the type of draw. In draws held through 2026 so far, general Canadian Experience Class cut-offs have sat roughly in the 507–523 range, French-language draws much lower (around 382–446), and provincial nominee draws far higher (around 697–805) because nominees carry 600 extra points. Treat these as weather, not promises — your job is to make your own score as strong as it can be.

The three levers that move a score most

  1. Language. Reaching CLB 9 in all four abilities of your first language both raises your language points and unlocks the top skill-transferability tiers. For many candidates this is worth more than a degree upgrade.
  2. A provincial nomination. 600 points, full stop. If your score is far below general cut-offs, a PNP stream matched to your occupation or province ties is usually the realistic route.
  3. French. Strong French (NCLC 7 or higher in all four abilities) adds 25–50 additional points, and French-language draws have had some of the lowest cut-offs of any category.
