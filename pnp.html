Provincial Nominee Programs (PNP) — the 600-point shortcut

Every province and territory except Nunavut can nominate people whose skills it needs. An Express Entry-linked nomination adds 600 CRS points — in practice, a guaranteed invitation in the next PNP draw.

Two kinds of streams

  • Enhanced (Express Entry) streams: you need an Express Entry profile; the nomination lands in it and adds 600 points.
  • Base streams: you apply to the province directly, outside Express Entry, and add the nomination to a paper/online PR application. Slower, but open to people who do not qualify for Express Entry.
Streams open, close and change criteria without much warning, and many require a job offer, work or study in the province, or a formal connection to it. Use this page to shortlist provinces — then read the province's own official pages before you commit.

Ontario — OINP

Canada's biggest provincial program. Well-known streams: Human Capital Priorities (Express Entry), Skilled Trades, French-Speaking Skilled Worker, and Employer Job Offer streams. Ontario also runs graduate streams for its own master's and PhD graduates.

British Columbia — BC PNP

Skills Immigration streams for skilled workers, healthcare professionals and entry-level workers, plus an Express Entry BC route. BC runs frequent draws, often targeted at tech, healthcare and childcare occupations.

Alberta — AAIP

The Alberta Advantage Immigration Program: Alberta Opportunity Stream for people already working in Alberta, an Express Entry stream, a Rural Renewal Stream for smaller communities, and sector streams such as tourism and hospitality.

Saskatchewan — SINP

Famous for its own points grid. International Skilled Worker routes include Express Entry and Occupation In-Demand sub-streams, plus a Saskatchewan Experience category for people already in the province.

Manitoba — MPNP

Skilled Worker in Manitoba and Skilled Worker Overseas streams, plus an International Education Stream. Manitoba values connections to the province — study, work, family or community support.

Nova Scotia — NSNP

Labour Market Priorities draws from the Express Entry pool for occupations the province needs, alongside employer-driven skilled worker routes. Streams open and pause — timing matters here.

New Brunswick — NBPNP

An Express Entry stream and a Skilled Worker stream built around New Brunswick employers, plus business immigration routes.

Prince Edward Island — PEI PNP

Express Entry, Labour Impact and Business Impact categories. A small program with regular draws.

Newfoundland and Labrador — NLPNP

Express Entry Skilled Worker and Skilled Worker categories, plus Priority Skills NL for in-demand occupations such as healthcare and technology.

Yukon, Northwest Territories & Nunavut

Yukon and the Northwest Territories run employer-driven nominee programs. Nunavut has no provincial nominee program.

How people actually choose a province

  1. Where can you work? Your occupation in demand beats a "nicer" province that never draws your job.
  2. What ties do you have? Study, work, family or a job offer in a province is the strongest signal most programs reward.
  3. Enhanced or base? If your CRS is close, an enhanced stream is faster. If you cannot enter Express Entry at all, look at base streams.
